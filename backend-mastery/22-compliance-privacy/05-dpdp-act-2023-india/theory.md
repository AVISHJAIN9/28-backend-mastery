# Theory – DPDP Act 2023 (India)

## What is the DPDP Act?
The **Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023** received Presidential assent on 11 August 2023. It is India's comprehensive data protection law, replacing the IT Act 2000's data protection provisions.

## Key Definitions
- **Data Principal**: The individual whose data is being processed (equivalent to GDPR "data subject")
- **Data Fiduciary**: Entity that determines purpose and means of processing (equivalent to GDPR "data controller")
- **Data Processor**: Entity that processes data on behalf of a Data Fiduciary
- **Significant Data Fiduciary (SDF)**: Designated by Central Government — faces additional obligations

## Core Obligations for Data Fiduciaries
1. **Consent (Section 6)**: Free, specific, informed, unconditional, unambiguous. Notice in all 22 scheduled languages.
2. **Purpose Limitation (Section 7)**: Process data only for the specified, legitimate purpose.
3. **Data Minimisation**: Collect only what is necessary.
4. **Accuracy**: Ensure data is accurate and complete.
5. **Storage Limitation**: Retain only as long as necessary.
6. **Security (Section 8)**: Implement appropriate technical and organisational measures.
7. **Breach Notification (Section 8(6))**: Notify the Data Protection Board and affected Data Principals.
8. **Grievance Redressal (Section 8(11))**: Must have a Grievance Officer accessible to Data Principals.

## Data Principal Rights
| Right | Section | SLA |
|-------|---------|-----|
| Access information about processing | 11 | 30 days |
| Correction and erasure | 12 | 30 days |
| Grievance redressal | 13 | 7 days |
| Nominate a representative | 14 | — |

## Additional SDF Obligations
- Appoint a Data Protection Officer (DPO) based in India
- Conduct Data Protection Impact Assessments (DPIA)
- Periodic audits by Data Auditor
- No cross-border transfer restrictions apply differently

## Cross-Border Transfers (Section 16)
Transfers only to countries whitelisted by the Central Government. The list is yet to be fully published (as of 2024).

## Penalties
- Breach of data security: up to ₹250 crore
- Failure to notify breach: up to ₹200 crore
- Breach of children's data obligations: up to ₹200 crore

## Comparison with GDPR
| Feature | GDPR | DPDP Act 2023 |
|---------|------|---------------|
| Lawful bases | 6 | Primarily consent + legitimate uses |
| Right to object | Yes | No direct equivalent |
| Right to data portability | Yes | No |
| DPO mandatory | For certain orgs | Only SDFs |
| Penalty | 4% global turnover | Up to ₹250 crore |
