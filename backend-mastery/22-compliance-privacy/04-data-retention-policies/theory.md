# Theory – Data Retention Policies

## Why Retain Data?
Organisations must balance two competing obligations:
1. **Delete data** that is no longer needed (GDPR Art. 5(1)(e) – storage limitation)
2. **Keep data** required by law (tax records, financial records, legal holds)

## Storage Limitation Principle (GDPR Art. 5(1)(e))
Personal data must be kept in a form which permits identification for no longer than necessary for the purpose for which it was collected. After that period: delete, anonymise, or archive (where a separate legal basis applies).

## Common Legal Retention Obligations (UK/India)
| Record Type | Obligation | Period |
|-------------|-----------|--------|
| Financial/Tax records | Companies Act / IT Act | 7–8 years |
| Employment records | Various | 6–7 years |
| Health records | NHS / Medical Council | 8–30 years |
| Legal correspondence | Limitation Act | 6–12 years |

## Delete vs. Anonymise vs. Archive
- **Delete**: Permanently remove from all systems including backups (within rotation cycle)
- **Anonymise**: Strip all identifying information so data can no longer be linked to an individual — anonymised data falls outside GDPR scope
- **Archive**: Move to cold storage with restricted access — still personal data, still subject to GDPR

## Legal Holds
When litigation or regulatory investigation is anticipated, normal retention schedules must be suspended for relevant data (litigation hold). Resume purge only once the hold is lifted.
